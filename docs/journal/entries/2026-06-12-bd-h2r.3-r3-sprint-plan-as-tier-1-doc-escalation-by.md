## What shipped

Two maintainer decisions reshaped the literal bead (authorized revisions, recorded
because they overrode the bead text):

1. **Plans are TIER-2 (reachable on-demand) + bead-linked, NOT tier-1.** The bead
   said "add the active sprint plan to `TIER1_DOCS`." The maintainer overrode it:
   with ~5 sprints in flight in parallel, always-loading every plan into every
   session is wrong -- "not every new session needs the full details of every
   sprint." This *also* dissolved a real tension: tier-1 docs are mechanically
   enforced current-state-only (no history chains), but sprint plans are inherently
   chronological (R1 -> R2 -> R3, A/B notes). As tier-2 reachable + bead-linked,
   plans keep their chronological shape and the clash disappears.

2. **Escalation-by-traceability SHARPENS CLAUDE.md step 2; it does not replace
   decide-by-default.** Traceability is a *lens* on the existing step-2 trigger
   ("beyond what the bead/plan authorizes -> escalate"), not a new gate requiring
   plan sign-off for every action. Implementation / internal structure / process
   mechanics stay decide-yourself (step 3). This deliberately avoids the
   over-escalation anti-pattern the doctrine already warns against.

## Lessons

- **A literal bead instruction can be the wrong design once you surface its
  tension.** "Promote plans to tier-1" collided with the current-state-only rule
  AND with the reality of many parallel sprints. Surfacing both tensions to the
  maintainer (rather than implementing the letter) produced a better shape
  (tier-2 + bead-linked). The traceability doctrine this bead *creates* is exactly
  the discipline that made escalating these two product calls correct.
- **Rebase before trusting `git diff origin/dev`.** Mid-bead, the diff stat showed
  ~8 "phantom deletions" (journal entries, telemetry, script edits) that were not
  mine -- my branch was 9 commits behind a fast-moving `dev` (PRs #172/#173 merged
  under me). A `git rebase origin/dev` made the diff show only my 7 files.
  On a busy repo, re-fetch + rebase before reading the diff or you misread merged
  work as your own deletions.
- **Check for an already-merged fix before building.** The `bd_safe.py` Windows
  npm-`bd` `.CMD` resolution bug I hit at claim time (filed `polyglot-pdf-ec6f`)
  was already fixed by the merged `ipnr` (PR #172, `swarm_config._NPM_BD_BIN`).
  Closed `ec6f` as a duplicate after verifying `bd_safe.py claim` works on the
  rebased base.
