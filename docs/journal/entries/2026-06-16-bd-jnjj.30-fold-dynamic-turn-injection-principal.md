## Trigger

Started as the implementation of jnjj.22 ("fix the JIT next-field drip"). The
investigation showed the bead was un-fixable as framed, which -- via Gemini's
"Dynamic Turn Injection" insight, refined by the maintainer -- redirected the
whole principal-side architecture. This entry captures the architectural finding
and the resulting plan/bead changes.

## What shipped

Docs/planning only (no code):

- **The JIT drip bug is un-fixable as framed.** In JIT mode the backend receives
  only ONE field's full schema (`websocket_routes.py` JIT branch:
  `fields=[first_field_schema]`); the manifest carries names only
  (`id|spoken_name`), so `get_next_field_schema` literally cannot produce the next
  field's type/options/validation. The full schema lives CLIENT-side
  (`getFieldSchema` / `state.schema.fields`) by the stateless-backend invariant.
  Every "repair" (send the whole section -> defeats JIT; per-field client
  round-trip -> latency + protocol; backend loads from disk -> breaks stateless)
  was worse than the disease.
- **Chosen direction (maintainer): replace the drip with Dynamic Turn Injection.**
  Keep the principal (live voice model) SI tiny + static (~behavioral rules, no
  catalog/schemas) and inject the CURRENT field's guidance as a `client_content`
  text turn before each question -- the same mechanism the cost harness already
  uses at section granularity (`switch_to_section` + activity brackets), finer per
  field. The broken schema-drip is deleted, not fixed.
- **Anti-accumulation = calibrated eviction (the l3a finding, inverted).** l3a
  found compression useless because the SI was huge and unevictable. With a tiny
  SI and guidance in evictable `client_content`, the maintainer's refinement is to
  size the compression window to ~the SI so each turn's guidance is
  deterministically evicted and re-injected fresh. This is a SECOND-ORDER
  calibration; the big win is the tiny principal SI + offloading catalog/value
  awareness to the secretary.
- **Plan + beads updated.** Folded the principal (injection) half into
  `docs/research/secretary-agent-research-plan.md` (now a unified principal +
  secretary design with a new Gate 1.5 for the two unverified claims). Reframed
  **jnjj.22** (fix-drip -> replace-with-injection, released back to open -- the
  BUILD is future work). Repurposed **jnjj.25** (G2-redo "JIT-as-built" ->
  "injection+secretary vs section-reset baseline on the real I-90").

## Lessons

- **A "bug" can be a signal that the mechanism is wrong, not broken.** jnjj.22
  looked like a one-line index fix; investigating it surfaced that the JIT
  schema-drip fights the stateless-backend invariant (client owns the schema, but
  the drip wants the backend to produce it). The right move was to delete the
  mechanism, not patch it. Investigate the data-ownership before "fixing"
  cross-tier plumbing.
- **External model suggestions get the same "measure, don't assume" treatment as
  our own.** Gemini's Dynamic Turn Injection architecture is strong and we adopted
  its shape -- but its two load-bearing claims (calibrated eviction works on 2.5
  native-audio; implicit caching keeps the sub-agent warm) are exactly the kind of
  genai-SDK behavior this project has been burned betting on. They are recorded as
  measured gates (Gate 1.5 + the secretary caching gate), not assumptions.
- **Keep the big win and the small fish separate.** The cost win is the tiny
  principal SI; the anti-accumulation mechanism (compression vs reset vs per-turn
  re-inject) is a second-order calibration. Naming that hierarchy keeps the
  research from over-investing in the small fish.
