## Trigger

Direct follow-up to hrn.11 (which first wrote the decide-by-default escalation
doctrine). The maintainer asked me to audit every `AskUserQuestion` use in the
preceding session against that doctrine and reconstruct, honestly, why each was
escalated rather than decided. The audit found **3 of 4 asks were
over-escalation.** The doctrine already *implied* the right answer in every case —
the gaps were in wording, not policy — so the maintainer scoped this as a **reword
of the existing CLAUDE.md section, not a new one.**

## What shipped

Five targeted rewords to CLAUDE.md "Escalation vs. autonomy", each tied to one
audited failure (calibration agreed with the maintainer: tighten against
over-asking but KEEP the soft-edge "when genuinely unsure, escalate" valve for
real commitments):

1. **Anti-pattern named up top** — `AskUserQuestion` is for decisions you're
   genuinely BLOCKED on, never for confirmation / sharing a reached call /
   transparency (that belongs in the defended-decision list at zero blocking
   cost). Includes the two poles the maintainer drew: (a) the LEGITIMATE
   clarification case — genuine low-confidence on what the maintainer meant in a
   *direct* request is a fair clarifying reply; vs (b) the TRAP — when a call is
   *delegated* to me ("whatever you think"), deciding it IS the response, and
   re-asking reads as not listening.
2. **No-stapling** — ask only the irreducible kernel; never bundle a
   decide-it-yourself choice into the same ask "while you're here."
3. **Scope-creep extension** — deciding *whether* out-of-scope work happens at all
   is a decide-or-file call, never an ask.
4. **Implementation FORM is mine** — doc vs. machine-readable config, file layout,
   section placement: when the bead is silent on the artifact's form, I pick the
   reversible default and note it.
5. **Consequence Test promoted** to a named rule + the clarifier that "no obvious
   default" + "no consequence beyond the sprint" still means *I pick* (reversible
   default), not escalate; plus a no-front-loading-questions-at-kickoff line (a
   question that arises before the work starts is almost never blocking yet).

The mapping of each audited failure → the rule that now catches it is also
recorded in my persistent escalation-doctrine memory (updated in place, not
duplicated).

## Lessons

- **My over-escalation tell is CONFIRMATION/TRANSPARENCY, not being blocked.**
  Three of four misses were me wanting a second opinion or to surface a call —
  exactly what the defended-decision list exists to absorb without a blocking stop.
- **Delegation ≠ permission to re-ask.** When the owner hands a technical call to
  the model, bouncing it back is the failure; deciding it is the response. This is
  distinct from clarifying genuine ambiguity in a direct request, which is fair.
  Naming BOTH poles in the doctrine keeps them from collapsing into each other.
- **A doctrine can be right and still under-perform on wording.** The fix here was
  zero policy change — just making the buried Test prominent and naming the
  anti-pattern explicitly. Worth remembering before reaching for a *new* rule.

**Fresh-eyes (Sonnet, arm B) round 1: 6 findings, all 6 adopted** — and they were
good, exactly the contradictions a doctrine reword risks introducing: (1) "no
obvious default" used in opposite directions in step 2 (escalate) vs. the Test
(decide) — fixed with an explicit order-of-operations (check step 2 first; the
Test only governs non-step-2 choices); (2) "Isolate the kernel" was structurally a
step-2 *trigger* when it's a *how-to-escalate* rule — reframed; (3) "never an ask"
contradicted the step-2 carve-out right above it (could have *weakened a
legitimate escalation*) — scoped to the low-consequence case with an explicit
"not a loophole around step 2"; (4) the consequence Test was a non-bulleted
paragraph easy to skip — made a proper bullet; (5) the "almost never blocking"
qualifier was an undefined loophole — named the one exception (a step-2 question
with zero independent work ahead of it); (6) no tiebreaker for the
ambiguous-between-direct-request-and-delegation case — added "treat as delegation
and decide." Zero deferred, zero rejected. No Copilot (docs-only, skipped per the
review-worthiness gate).
