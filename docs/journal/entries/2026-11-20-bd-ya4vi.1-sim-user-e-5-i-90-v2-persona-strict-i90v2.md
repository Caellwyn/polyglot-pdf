## Trigger

The maintainer dropped gemini-live-2.5 from the sim-user measurement
(2026-11-20: 2.5 is being sunset). They asked instead to redo every
gemini-3.8-live benchmark group the scripted user had tainted, so the 3.8
cost and performance picture is accurate.

The 3.8 benchmark (bd `e72on`, `3p8_cost_bench_findings.md`) had three such
groups:
- W-7 happy;
- W-7 "unhappy", with every answer corrected once;
- I-90 v2, where all three runs derailed.

The sim user could only run W-7 with a persona that never corrects itself, so
two groups had no sim equivalent. This change adds both. The measurement
itself is bd `polyglot-pdf-15j9v`.

## What shipped

- **Per-schema sim mode.**
  - `SIM_SCHEMAS = ("w7", "i90-v2")`.
  - `resolve_sim_persona` picks the per-form default persona
    (`actor.DEFAULT_PERSONA_FILES`).
  - `validate_sim_args` requires the persona's form to equal `--schema`.
- **E.5: I-90 v2 persona** `i90v2_elena_nospell_v1`.
  - It has 57 facts, in `I90V2_QUESTIONS` order, for the same fictional
    Elena Torres the scripted run uses, and it does not spell unprompted.
  - The physical-address answer stays the natural "same as my mailing
    address". That is the bd `r3ham.10` behavior under test, so it was not
    reworded away.
- **The strict `I90V2_EXPECTED` key.** The empty I-90 v2 key now has 57 strict
  values:
  - option slugs;
  - `""` for declined fields;
  - any-of lists for country and place spellings;
  - the A-Number with or without its leading zero, because USCIS pads
    8-digit numbers and both forms are the same number.

  I-90 v2 now scores strictly in scripted runs too. Until now it was lenient
  and counted any non-empty value as correct, so earlier I-90 v2 "accuracy"
  figures are not comparable with new ones.
- **E.6: slips.**
  - A fact may carry a validated `slip` (`value`, `answer`, `spoken`): a
    plausible wrong value.
  - The first `answer` for a slipped field speaks the slip, and only an
    actually-injected turn marks it spoken.
  - The actor never sees the slip: not in the prompt, the history or the
    judging. So it rejects the wrong read-back and corrects through the
    existing moves.
  - A slip is fact-sheet text, so it is never off-sheet. A field left
    holding its slip scores incorrect.
  - `summary.sim_user.corrections` reports slips spoken and corrected,
    fields left uncorrected, and reject-and-correct turns.
- **Persona `w7_ana_selfcorrect_v1`:** every W-7 fact is slipped once, the
  sim equivalent of the scripted unhappy path.
  - The first-name slip is "Alma", not "Anna", because gTTS says Ana and Anna
    identically (bd `r3ham.14`).
  - The "previously received an ITIN: yes" slip deliberately opens a
    conditional branch that the actor has to back out of.
- **Tests:** drift tests pin every persona to its strict key, plus
  fake-session runs for both personas. All ten mutations went red and were
  restored byte-for-byte.

## Lessons

- **A mutation script that silently skips a pattern looks like a pass.** Two
  of the builder's ten mutations were "NO-OP (pattern missing)". They proved
  nothing until they were re-run by hand, and both then went red. Count the
  applied mutations, not just the red ones.
- **A test that loads a fixture at import and again at run time is racy
  against an edit between the two loads.** It failed once, mid-edit, in a
  full-suite run, then passed three times in a row; it was not flaky.
- **A strict key must encode equivalences the domain treats as identical**
  (the A-Number leading zero). Otherwise the strict scorer reports
  persona-formatting noise as model error.
